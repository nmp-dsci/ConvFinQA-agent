s35 · ConvFinQA-agent · 2026-11-12 · findings · DPO round 3, coverage of the whole pool at 8 tries · branch dpo

Eight tries over all 1,489 conversations give 2,448 capped pairs. They come from 619 questions in 520 conversations; the other 969 conversations give none.

sft2-e1 answered every reachable question of dpo_v1 8 times. The first 300 conversations got 4 more tries each on rebuilt histories, and the other 1,189 got 8 fresh. Single-try accuracy is 81.3%. At 8 tries, 18.7% of the questions reached get both a right and a wrong try, against 11.4% at 4. With every right × wrong combination allowed, a cap of 4 per question and the strict chosen check, that gives 2,448 pairs: 2,348 train and 100 eval. That is three times s33's P3 gate of 800. The pair selection is written; rendering and the trainer come next.

pass@k is the unbiased estimate from the 8 tries (1 − C(n−c,k)/C(n,k)), over the 3,895 reached questions. Each question is asked on a history whose earlier answers were right, so these figures are per question given a right walk, not per conversation. The gap from pass@1 (81.3%) to pass@8 (88.8%) is the headroom DPO is aiming at: the student can produce the right answer on 7.5pp more questions than it picks it on. By position, pass@8 is 85.8% at q0, 91.2% at q1, 91.0% at q2 and 87.8% at q3. Majority vote adds only 1.4pp, because when the student is wrong it tends to be wrong the same way.

03b

All wrong @8: the 438 questions a backfill would add

These questions had 0 right out of 8 graded tries. Each one ended its conversation, so the 499 questions after them were never asked. A backfill would supply a chosen for each of them, pair it against the student's 8 wrong tries, and then sample on into the questions behind it. They are 357 program and 81 number turns, 416 train and 22 eval, at q0 212, q1 112, q2 68 and q3 46. The full list is in evaluation/dpo/v1/all_wrong_tries8.json, with each question's gold and its top 3 answers.

walking on

≈ 310 more

From sampling the 499 unreached questions at 8 tries, at the pool's rate (15.9% of reached questions paired, 3.95 pairs each). About 55 of them would come back all wrong again. That is about an hour of L4 time.

the catch

Off-policy chosen

Except for "wrong form", the chosen would not be the student's own text. DPO on a chosen the policy finds unlikely tends to lower the likelihood of both sides, which is why s33's D2 left the rescue out and why the pilot runs without it. The NLL term on the chosen is the usual guard.

Across all reached questions, 914 of 31,127 wrong tries (2.9%) are within 1% of gold in another form. So the decimal-for-percent habit is a steady cost on pass@1, not only on these 97. A format pair (own try vs the same try restated) would target it directly.

06

The cap: what each setting yields

Selection is pairs.select_capped. Any clean right try may pair with any clean wrong try. Picks are greedy: first the least-used wrong answer, then the least-used rejected try, then the least-used chosen try. Strict also requires the chosen to be within 1% of gold (at ×1, ×100 or ×0.01) or equal to gold at gold's precision. Strict drops 9 questions here; the eval metric is unchanged.

why 4

Every paired question counts the same

At cap 4 the top tenth of paired questions holds 10.0% of the pairs, which is flat. With no cap, a 4/8 question would give 16 pairs and a 1/8 question 7, so the questions the student is unsure of would count more than the ones it nearly has.

enough

About three times the gate

2,348 train pairs, against s33's P3 gate of ≥ 800 and the pilot's ≈ 400. That is below the RLHF book's 6.4k config and Step-DPO's 10k, but in the same range, and spread over 619 distinct questions.

the limit

Repeats, not new questions

Going from cap 4 to cap 8 adds 1,913 pairs and no new question, and every added pair repeats a try already used. More distinct questions come only from the 438 all-wrong questions and the 499 never reached (§03).
