02 · Statistical power

How many questions a promotion needs before "net positive" means anything

The gate is a paired comparison: the same questions, champion vs challenger, McNemar's test on the flips. Its power depends on two numbers we already observe: the flip rate ψ (fixed + broken as a share of n) and the net effect Δ (fixed − broken). v3_1 → v5 on test: ψ = 20/187 = 10.7%, Δ = +2.1pp. A challenger that breaks ~4% of questions as collateral (8/187, observed) must fix 4% + Δ, so ψ ≈ 8.6% + Δ.

The v5 promotion had 14% power to detect its own effect.

At n = 187 a 5pp improvement is detected less than half the time (47%); a 2pp one, one time in seven. The v3_1 and v4 promotions (n = 44) were sized to detect ~14pp. Every promotion so far is plausible, none is demonstrated — which is exactly what the "not significant at α = 0.05" flags recorded.

Questions cluster inside conversations

Flips are not independent: a wrong turn poisons the turns below it. Measured on the v3_1/v5 test CSVs, the within-report intraclass correlation of the flip indicator is 0.20 (broken turns 0.24, fixed 0.09), with 3.74 questions per report — a design effect of 1.56×. The cluster-adjusted columns below divide the sample by that; the naive column is what a question-level McNemar believes.

03 · Split roles & holdout policy

The gate split decides the champion; the holdout is reserved for later deferred

Decision: no holdout runs during this phase.

The campaign optimises and promotes on the gate split alone. The holdout is not cut, not run, and not opened — it exists as the untouched remainder of the pool, from which a sealed split can be drawn whenever you want a confirmatory number. Nothing about deferring it burns it, provided the gate split is never resampled from the reserve.

Worth being clear about what that means for the claim. With no holdout, the campaign's headline is "accuracy on a fixed 363-question gate split, improved from X% to Y% over N promotions, each significant at p < 0.05" — an honest and defensible claim, and the significance gate is doing real work in it. What it is not is an out-of-sample claim: after a dozen challengers have been measured against the same questions, that split is no longer unseen, and a portion of any gain is selection.

That is why the reserve matters more than a schedule. The single most valuable thing you can do at the end — one run, ~$4, 21 minutes — is cut a fresh 270-report holdout from the untouched reserve and run the final champion and the starting champion on it. Two numbers, never optimised against, that convert the whole campaign into an out-of-sample result. The plan therefore keeps release.py as-is and simply does not call it until then.

Should the splits be resampled every run?

The instinct is right, and the answer is different for the two splits — because they are doing different statistical jobs.

yes train — resample every cycle

Nothing about diagnosis needs stability. Drawing a fresh 100 reports from the reserve each cycle gives the teacher new failure material, stops it tuning to the quirks of one fixed set, and widens the evidence behind each prompt rewrite over the campaign. Strictly better, and free.

Record train_seed and the drawn report_id list on the run so the cycle is reproducible.

Drawn from the reserve only — never from the gate split. The gate ids are subtracted from the sampling frame before any draw, so no gate report can ever appear in train. This is the one invariant that, if broken, silently invalidates every promotion in the campaign: the teacher would be writing prompts against the exact questions the gate then scores.

no gate — must stay fixed

The promotion test is paired: the same question answered by champion and challenger, McNemar on the flips. Pairing is what makes 100 reports viable at all. Resample the gate and there is nothing to pair — you fall back to comparing two independent samples, and the same data buys far less.

Same 363 questions: paired detects 6.2pp, unpaired 9.2pp. To recover 6.2pp unpaired you need ~234 reports per arm — 2.3× the data, $3.57 and 18 min per challenger instead of $1.52 and 8 min.

But the worry behind the question is real.

A fixed gate split measured against twenty challengers does accumulate selection — that is exactly the concern that resampling would solve. The resolution is not to resample the gate, which trades a large power loss for it, but to keep the gate fixed and settle the accumulated selection once, at the end, on a freshly drawn sample from the untouched reserve. That is the confirmatory run described above, and this is the argument for actually budgeting it: a fixed gate plus one clean out-of-sample check is both more powerful per cycle and more honest at the end than resampling every cycle.

Reproducibility, either way.

Adopt the seed discipline you describe for both splits. The manifest already stores literal report_id lists rather than trusting a seed to reproduce (the old machinery is the cautionary tale — two 60/40 splits both seeded 42 agree on only 78 of 120 conversations). Extend that: each run logs manifest, split, seed, and the drawn report ids as an MLflow artifact, so any cycle can be re-created exactly even after the train pool has moved on.

Out-of-scope reports resolved — no filter

Settled: there is no out-of-scope exclusion to build. The pool is the 3,198 conversations the old optimisers never saw, and train and gate are simply sampled down from it — 100 reports each. Whatever unanswerable cases exist stay in the data at their natural rate, which is the honest thing for a benchmark: they sit in the champion's accuracy as a ceiling rather than being quietly removed to flatter it.

Two consequences worth naming. First, both arms of every paired comparison contain the same unanswerable reports, so they cancel in Δ — the gate is unaffected. Second, the teacher will occasionally diagnose a case whose gold is itself wrong; the gold_suspect flag already exists for exactly that, and /admin/dataset is where those get settled by hand. Watch the flag rate per cycle; if it climbs, that is the signal to revisit. W0b is dropped and Slice 1 has no blockers.

Program-question filter resolved — no change

Checked: all 3,198 pool conversations already contain at least one Program question (2,333 have exactly two; only 19 have one), so a "≥ 1 Program" filter excludes nothing — ConvFinQA conversations are decomposed multi-hop questions by construction. Program turns are 65.8% of pool questions and remain where the failures are (v5: 70.7% vs 96.9% on Number), so the campaign targets them via the accuracy_gold_turn_type_Program slice rather than by narrowing the data.

Stopping a conversation after its first wrong answer

Measured on the real runs, the saving is 24% of turns on train and 13% on the gate split — the cascade turns that currently run only to be discarded. On train that is straightforwardly worth taking.

yes train — stop at first wrong

The teacher reads exactly one case per report: the first wrong turn. Everything after it is computed, paid for, and thrown away. Stopping there cuts ~24% of turns off every train pass at zero information cost — a cycle's train run drops from ~$1.52 to ~$1.15 and 8 min to ~6.

no gate — must run every turn

The gate compares champion and challenger question by question. Stop early and the two runs stop at different turns, so the shared question set shrinks to whatever both happened to reach — the pairing breaks, the sample shrinks unpredictably, and a challenger that fails earlier would be scored on fewer questions than the champion.

Worse, it is biased: truncating after the first error removes precisely the cascade turns a better prompt would have recovered, so a genuine improvement becomes invisible.

So: --stop-at-first-wrong, defaulted on for train and refused for the gate split.

A per-run flag rather than a global behaviour, with the runner rejecting it when --split test so it cannot be set by accident. Cascade turns stay in the gate's CSV, where cascade already marks them and the story page uses them to show error propagation shrinking.

Deferred, not abandoned.

Two changes to release.py are worth making when the confirmatory run happens, not before: reuse the baseline's recorded holdout CSV rather than re-running it, and report the cluster-bootstrap CI alongside the "must not regress" check. Both are noted in W3.

Campaign = a capped series of experiments decided

Optimisation runs periodically rather than continuously, so the unit of work is a campaign: a named, bounded series of experiments against one fixed gate split, capped at 5 experiments. When a campaign ends you review it as a whole, and the next campaign starts deliberately — new gate split if you want one, new baseline, new name.

Both caps are enforced in the cycle command, not left to discipline: it refuses a 6th experiment in a campaign, and refuses a target that has just failed twice in a row.

Standing rules for the campaign

  • One subagent per challenger, always — but unrestricted within it. The teacher may rewrite that agent's prompt from scratch if it wants. The prompt diff between consecutive champions is then still the entire explanation of the move, which is what makes the story page legible.
  • Train never touches the gate split. Every train draw comes from pool − gate, asserted in code. If a gate report ever reached train, the teacher would be tuning prompts on the very questions the gate scores, and every promotion after it would be worthless.
  • The gate split is fixed for the campaign; train is resampled every cycle. The gate must never be resampled — the paired test needs the same questions, and pairing is what makes 100 reports viable. Train should be resampled, so the teacher keeps seeing new failures.
  • Failed challengers stay on the page. They are the honest half of the story and the teacher's best evidence.
  • Five experiments per campaign, then review. At one-sided α = 0.05 that is 0.25 expected false promotions if every challenger were null — small enough that a campaign's result stands on its own.

The teacher's latitude decided

Today the teacher is constrained twice over, and both constraints come out. In teacher.py the diagnosis step asks for "ONE imperative prompt rule" per failure (RulesBlock), and the prompt_writer then merges those rules into the target agent's prompt. It can add guidance; it cannot reorganise, shorten, or replace what is already there — so prompts only ever grow, and a prompt whose structure is the problem can never be fixed.

The teacher reads the first wrong answer per report_id, and may rewrite the target agent's system prompt however it judges best.

It receives the full current prompt, the diagnosed failures, and the outcome ledger of its own prior attempts on that agent (below), and returns a complete replacement prompt — free to reorder, restructure, delete, or rewrite from scratch. Then the experiment runs and the gate decides. The teacher proposes; only the p < 0.05 comparison promotes.

  • Still one agent per challenger. Unrestricted within that agent, untouched everywhere else — so the diff between consecutive champions remains a single agent's prompt, which is what the story page needs to explain a move.
  • Guardrails that stay: the output must be a valid prompt for that agent (the pipeline's contract — required output fields, the DSL — is validated before the run), and a rewrite that collapses the prompt to nothing simply fails the gate.
  • What it costs: bigger diffs are harder to read than a one-line rule. The story page shows a full unified diff per promotion, and the teacher is asked for a one-paragraph rationale recorded on the propose run — that rationale becomes the caption on the story page's promotion card.

Why this matters more than it looks: attribution picks the target, and the campaign is capped at 5 experiments.

Every experiment changes the subagent with the most attributed faults. If attribution is a coin-flip-plus, a meaningful share of experiments optimise the wrong agent — and with only 5 per campaign, one or two misdirected experiments is the difference between a campaign that finds something and one that does not. This was invisible while the loop was a demo; it is the first thing that would have quietly wasted the campaign.

Decision: gold-derived attribution becomes primary; the teacher explains and proposes rather than attributes.
  • Target selection uses the deterministic counts. Free, reproducible, and it cannot drift.
  • The teacher keeps the job only it can do — why the agent failed and what to change. It is told the computed attribution rather than asked for one, which also shortens its prompt.
  • It may dissent, and dissent is recorded. The deterministic rule has known looseness — a program that is correct but shaped differently reads as a preprocess miss (the docstring flags this). When the teacher disagrees, the case is logged as attribution_disputed; a recurring dispute pattern is a bug in the rule, and the disagreement rate becomes a tracked metric.

What the teacher remembers — and the gap that matters for a long campaign

It does have memory, and here is exactly what it is. teacher.py:166 prior_diagnoses() queries MLflow for the last 5 runs tagged kind=diagnose in the convfinqa-optimization experiment, downloads each one's diagnoses.jsonl artifact, and appends up to 40 entries to the diagnosis prompt under the header "Prior diagnoses (do not repeat these rules)". So the teacher does carry across runs — within that one experiment — and n_prior_diagnoses is logged on every diagnose run so you can see how much memory it had.

Two gaps, both now closed by decision below.
  • The prompt writer has no memory at all. propose_version() passes only the current prompt and the fresh diagnoses to prompt_writer — nothing about what it already tried. With free-rewrite latitude and a p < 0.05 gate, it will produce rewrite after rewrite with no knowledge of the ones that were already rejected, and can happily re-propose a variant that failed three cycles ago.
  • Memory carries diagnoses, never outcomes. What is fed back is what was attributed and proposed — never whether the resulting challenger passed or failed. The teacher cannot learn which of its rules actually worked, because it is never told. That is memory without feedback, and it is the single biggest limiter on the loop improving over a long campaign.

Also raise the memory caps — 5 runs / 40 entries was sized for a handful of cycles, not a campaign — and key diagnosis memory by target agent so a preprocess rewrite sees preprocess history first.

The promotion rule decided

A challenger promotes only when it is significantly better: one-sided cluster-corrected McNemar, α = 0.05, on the shared gate questions.

Net-positive is retired. promotable stops meaning "fixed > broken" and starts meaning "fixed > broken and the paired test rejects no-difference in the improvement direction at α = 0.05". The p that gate.py already computes becomes the gate instead of a footnote. The two-sided p and the cluster bootstrap CI are still recorded on every verdict.

Two refinements the implementation has to get right, because the naive test is wrong in this data's shape:

  • Cluster-adjust the test. Flips cluster inside conversations (ICC 0.20) — a wrong turn poisons the turns below it — so treating 363 questions as 363 independent pairs over-states evidence and inflates false promotions. Use the cluster-corrected McNemar (Durkalski: Z = Σ dₖ / √Σ dₖ² over reports, report = cluster). On the v5 data: naive p = 0.502, clustered p = 0.414 — same verdict here, but the correction is what keeps α honest at scale. The cluster bootstrap CI is reported alongside as the effect size.
  • The target metric is evidence, not a second gate. The changed subagent's deterministic metric (e.g. acc_preprocess_skeleton) still has to move in the right direction and is recorded on the verdict — it is how the story page explains why the number moved — but it can no longer promote on its own. One rule decides: overall p.
Consequence: the gate split size is the smallest promotable improvement.

A significance gate cannot promote what it cannot detect. At 80% power, cluster-adjusted, with the observed ~4% collateral break rate:

Historically your single-agent changes have landed between +2.1pp and +9.1pp, so at 363 questions only the largest improvements promote and the rest are correctly described as "not demonstrated" — rejected, re-diagnosed, and retried rather than banked. That is a defensible campaign, and a stronger claim on the story page than a longer list of unproven promotions. It is why D1b in section 02 is the decision that most shapes this campaign.

Would α = 0.10 detect more? Yes — but there is a strictly better way to buy the same thing

Loosening α does help, and the amount is modest: at 100 reports a two-sided test moves from 7.3pp to 6.2pp. The cost is that the false-promotion rate doubles by construction.

A one-sided test at α = 0.05 detects exactly what a two-sided test at α = 0.10 detects — 6.2pp at 100 reports — with none of the extra false-promotion risk. And it is the honest description of what the gate does: it only ever promotes improvements, so spending half the rejection region on "significantly worse" is wasted. A challenger that is significantly worse is rejected either way.

Framed as: promote when the challenger is significantly better, one-sided, α = 0.05, cluster-corrected. The two-sided p and the bootstrap CI are still recorded on every verdict.

With the 5-experiment campaign cap, the false-promotion arithmetic is comfortable.

If every challenger in a campaign were truly null, expected false promotions are 0.25 at α = 0.05 and 0.50 at α = 0.10 across 5 experiments. Capping campaigns is what makes a modest α defensible — the earlier ≤ 20-challenger figure was the thing that made it uncomfortable.

One thing the rule should not do: let a rejected challenger be re-run until it passes.

Re-running the same challenger on the same gate split and taking the best of three is how a 5% α becomes a 14% one. The cycle records every attempt; a challenger gets one gated run, and a re-proposal must change the prompt.

The demo lineage is rolled back decided

v3_1, v4 and v5 were promoted on demo-scale evidence under a rule that did not require significance. None of them is a measured result, so none of them stands as a champion. The champion alias returns to v2 and the campaign starts from a clean slate.

  • Nothing is deleted. prompts/v3_1.py, v4.py and v5.py stay in the repo and their per-agent components stay in the lineage. They become unproven candidates: if v5's preprocess rewrite was genuinely good, the teacher can re-derive or re-propose it, and it will earn its place at one-sided p < 0.05 like anything else.
  • The rollback is recorded, not silent. One registry.promote(force=True, reason=…) naming the demo-evidence protocol change, exactly as the 2026-09-02 v4 rollback was recorded.
  • The story page starts here. Its first data point is a measured baseline, not a backfilled number — so everything the page claims is earned inside the campaign.
Worth $4.50 and 24 minutes: pick the starting point with data rather than by convention.

Defaulting to v2 assumes v2 is the best of the unproven options, which is no more measured than assuming v5 is. The champion has to be run on the new gate split once anyway to establish the baseline — so run all three (v2, v3_1, v5) in that same pass, 3 × $1.52, ~24 min, and start from whichever is actually strongest with the paired comparisons recorded. If v5 wins on real evidence it earns the alias legitimately; if v2 wins, you have avoided starting a 15-cycle campaign from a regression. Settled once, with numbers, before any cycle runs.

Risks and open questions

Test-set tuning over many cycles.

Twenty challengers against one gate split will eventually promote noise, and with no holdout during the campaign nothing catches it until the end. Mitigations: the one-sided p < 0.05 rule, the ≤ 20 cap, one gated run per challenger, and the end-of-campaign confirmatory run on untouched reserve.

Non-determinism at temperature.

Re-running the same version gives different CSVs (v3_1 train10: 61.4 / 63.6 / 63.6%). Paired comparison absorbs most of it; the page should show a same-version re-run as the noise floor once (one extra $1.50 pass — at this split size that floor is essential context).

Teacher usage is not logged, in either world.

Diagnose and propose runs record no usage at all today — only pipeline turns reach the trace store. On deepseek-v4-pro that is unrecorded dollars ($0.55/$2.19 per M tokens); after W1c it is unrecorded subscription consumption, which still matters because ~55 cases a cycle × 5 experiments is the campaign's real throughput limit. W2 captures token counts either way and reports them beside, never inside, the per-question pipeline cost.

Serving drift.

Promotion moves the alias; the served bundle must follow (demo_smoke.sh asserts served == champion). Each promotion in the campaign should end with a snapshot + a container rebuild, or the public demo lags the page.

Open: the headline claim.

Without a holdout the honest headline is "X% → Y% on a fixed 363-question gate split, over N promotions each significant at one-sided p < 0.05". If the end-of-campaign confirmatory run happens, it becomes an out-of-sample claim instead — much stronger, and worth the $4.

Open: rejected-but-real changes.

At 363 questions a genuine +3pp improvement will be rejected, repeatedly. Should the teacher's memory keep it as a candidate to re-propose in combination later, or is it discarded? Combining two rejected single-agent changes breaks the one-agent-per-challenger rule that makes the story page legible.
