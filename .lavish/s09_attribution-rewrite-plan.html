01 — Why this is on the table

In s08 I traced seven turns where the pipeline answered the literal string "no" to a numeric question, and argued the calculator was at fault. You pushed back: the "no" is a symptom, and the real question is whether the retriever failed to find a value that was there, or preprocess asked for the wrong value in the first place.

Checking that settled it. Every gold operand in all seven cases is present in the source document — there is no case where the data was genuinely missing, so no refusal was ever justified. And when I attributed the seven by hand, I disagreed with the shipped attribution on five.

The failure is not in the calculator prompt, it is in the diagnosis.

Attribution is what pick_target ranks on, so a systematically wrong attribution sends every experiment in a campaign at the wrong agent. Five experiments have now run against it. Fixing the calculator prompt would have been a sixth wasted one.

02 — What the rule does today, and its three defects

evalloop/stage_scores.py::first_fault walks four gold-derived checks in pipeline order and returns the first that fails. The order is the pipeline's, which is right. The checks are not.

defect 1

Plan shape is judged before operand coverage

A skeleton mismatch short-circuits before the retriever check ever runs, so any turn with an unusual plan is filed as preprocess regardless of what was actually retrieved. Preprocess wins ties it should lose.

defect 2

Skeleton equality is invalid for a symbolic plan

pred_program is symbolic — divide(A, B), with placeholders bound to sub-questions. Comparing its op list to gold's punishes every correct plan that is shaped differently, and passes wrong plans that happen to share a shape.

defect 3

Every case must name an agent

attribute() falls back to "calculator" when nothing fails. There is no way to say the gold label is wrong or this is not decidable from the record, so both are silently charged to an innocent agent.

Defect 2, concretely

That bucket — 41 of 554, 7.4% — is where the teacher earns its cost, on one binary question instead of open-ended attribution.

Why operand coverage moves ahead of the plan check

It is not merely a reordering — it follows from the pipeline. Preprocess emits both the plan and the sub-questions, and the plan is symbolic: its placeholders only acquire values once the retriever answers. So the plan literally cannot be evaluated until coverage is known. The old order asked a question it did not yet have the inputs to answer, and answered it with a proxy.

05 — The change, in code

The whole rewrite lands in one function. Helpers (retrieved_pairs, missing_operands, retriever_declined, plan_reaches_gold) are new and pure; score_rows and the per-agent metric panel are untouched, so the deterministic metrics gate-targeted judges against do not move.

src/convfinqa/evalloop/stage_scores.py — attribution block only

The one new piece of real logic

plan_reaches_gold is what replaces skeleton equality. It binds pred_program’s placeholders to the retriever’s answers in sub-question order, executes the DSL, and compares the result to the gold answer. It returns None — not False — when the plan cannot be bound, because “I could not read this” and “this plan is wrong” are different claims and only one of them should cost an agent a fault.

There is no DSL executor in the repo today.

evaluation/metrics.py parses and normalises programs but never runs one; the pipeline executes through tool calls in pipeline/tools.py. This adds a ~25-line evaluator over the six ops, which is also independently useful for the dataset page and for gold_suspect triage.

08 — What this breaks downstream

Attribution is not a leaf. Four things read it, and changing it invalidates history in one of them.

pick_target — behaviour change, intended

ledger.fault_history and the Wilson lower bound are unchanged; they simply receive different counts. Non-agent verdicts must be dropped from both numerator and denominator, or gold_suspect silently inflates every agent’s fault rate.

ledger history — must be recomputed, not migrated

Recorded fault counts were produced by the old rule. Mixing them with new counts under one Wilson bound compares two different measurements. A backfill-attribution command should re-derive every historical run from its committed CSV — the same shape as backfill-flips, and like it, it should refuse to write when a run’s CSV is missing.

The teacher — smaller, sharper job

It is currently told the attribution and may dissent, with an attribution_reason. Under the new rule it should be asked a binary question on the ~7% ambiguous bucket only: does any preprocess sub-question ask for this missing operand? That is a far easier judgement than open attribution, where it agreed with gold on only 17 of 30.

gate-targeted — unaffected

It judges the target on its deterministic metric from score_rows, which this does not touch. Attribution is only its fallback. The per-agent panel and every promotion verdict on record stand.

Two of five campaign experiments were probably aimed by a wrong attribution.

c01-e03 and c02-e01 both targeted the retriever. Re-scoring their train draws moves the retriever from 18 to 7 and from 15 to 6 faults respectively, which would very likely have put preprocess top on the Wilson bound in both. Both experiments were rejected at the gate. That is not proof they were misaimed — but it is the cheapest available explanation, and it is consistent with c02-e01 gating at exactly +0.00pp.

09 — Risks and open questions

risk

Preprocess becomes the target almost every cycle

305 of 554 — 55% — land on preprocess. The Wilson bound will pick it repeatedly, and the campaign’s rotate-after-two-failures rule becomes the only thing keeping the other three agents reachable. That rule was written as a safety valve, not as the primary scheduler. Mitigation: keep it, and watch whether preprocess’s share falls as its prompt improves — the denominator is first-wrong cases, so it should.

risk

The document test is a substring search

Double_ECL proves the weakness: gold’s 131 is a mangled column header, present as characters but not as a value, so the case escapes gold_suspect into adjudication. Only 4 of 554 currently trip the test at all. Mitigation: tokenise numerically rather than by substring, and accept that this check finds clear-cut label errors only.

no ground truth

The new rule is validated against seven hand-labelled cases

That is enough to show the old rule is wrong and far too little to claim the new one is right. Mitigation: the kappa command already exists for teacher-vs-human agreement; extend its sheet to carry a human attribution label and score both rules against ~30 labelled cases before this drives any targeting.

separate finding

4% of failures are a ratio graded against a percentage

22 of 554 wrong turns are numerically right and formatted wrong — 0.7824… against a gold of 78%; numeric_match returns False. Arguably correct (the answer format is wrong) but it means a slice of “calculator” faults are a formatting convention, not arithmetic. Not folded into this plan — it changes scored accuracy and deserves its own decision.

Open questions

  1. Should an unbindable plan count against preprocess? 18 cases are unscorable because more sub-questions were planned than the retriever answered. That asymmetry is arguably itself a preprocess signal (Double_STZ is exactly this: four asks, two answerable). Charging it would move ~3% from nothing to preprocess; leaving it keeps the verdict honest but discards evidence.
  2. What happens to ambiguous when the teacher is unavailable? Diagnosis runs on the Agent SDK on your subscription. If a cycle runs without it, the choices are default-to-preprocess (your stated prior, and it would make the rule fully deterministic) or exclude the bucket entirely (smaller denominator, no invented evidence).
  3. Do we re-score the campaign history before or after the next cycle? Re-scoring first makes c03 comparable to a corrected past; re-scoring later keeps the current champion’s provenance untouched but leaves the ledger mixing two rules.
