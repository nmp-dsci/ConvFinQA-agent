03

Would another teacher run pay? Barely: ≈ +12–25 questions

why so little

The misses are stable, not random

In 276 of 334 misses the teacher lands on the student's exact wrong number, often one the student gave 8 times out of 8. Two models agreeing on one wrong value is a fixed misreading (or a bad label), and re-sampling repeats it. Buckets 2–5 (152 questions) can't be fixed by retrying at all: the "error" is in gold or the scorer.

the estimate

Only bucket 6 can move

The 40 genuine misses where the teacher differs from the student are the unstable ones; at p ≈ 0.2–0.35 per retry that's +8–14. The 142 where it agrees, at p ≈ 0.03–0.08, add +4–11. So a second run likely adds about +12–25 (3–6% of 438), and 8 runs perhaps +40–60 in total. Upper bound if every miss were independent: +66.

A second run costs about $1 and 9 minutes, so it's cheap to measure rather than estimate, and it would firm up the per-question rates above. It isn't a strategy for the rest, though: 182 + 33 questions need a writer that is given the answer.
