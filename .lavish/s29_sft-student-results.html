Fine-tuning Qwen3-4B on llm_react_v1's own conversations took it from 54% to 72% on val, and from 56% to 77.4% on the unseen test 100.

This is the first end-to-end run of the s28 plan. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash answered the 552 sft_v1 train conversations in its own harness (llm_react_v1), and the 334 it got right on every turn became training rows. tau's Qwen3-4B student was LoRA-tuned on them for three epochs on Modal. Each epoch was scored on 100 val conversations, epoch 2 was picked, and only then was it scored once on the test 100. The second half of the page reads every val failure, checks the SFT input for what is missing, and sets out the next fine-tune, which starts from epoch 2 with more data.

How to read the gain. Most of it is the student learning the harness: answering through a tool call, calling the calculator, and keeping the conversation's state. The base student lost 96 of its 365 val turns to errors, 94 of them "no valid reply after 2 attempts", mostly from writing its answer as text; epoch 2 lost one. Almost all of the rest is program turns (39.7% → 65.4% on val); number turns barely moved (80.2% → 83.2%). On test the student closes two thirds of the gap to its teacher. The rest is mostly finding the right value, unit and sign in the report, and errors that carry into later turns (§05); about 14 of its 45 first-wrong test turns are gold or convention cases no student fix moves.

A cross-cutting habit

Arithmetic answered as a lookup

Every arithmetic turn in training calls the calculator. On val, 27 arithmetic turns were answered with no calculator call: 12 right, 15 wrong. 9 of the 46 first-wrong turns carry this as a secondary tag. The student learned the calculator well but sometimes classifies a "change", "sum" or "portion" question as a number turn.

Errors carry forward

57 later wrong turns from 46 first mistakes

Only 20 of the 77 turns after a first error are right. Printed minus signs

12 answers are exact sign flips (6 of 46 on val). Six come from copying a minus the table prints on a ratio, liability, tax benefit or loss into the arithmetic. new on test

Three-number sums crash the harness

4 test turns errored: the student passed a third argument to add for a three-number sum or average, and the harness raised a TypeError instead of returning a tool error. No training row has a 3-argument call or program, yet epoch 2 writes add(A, B, C) in 10 test and 9 val programs: it generalised a shape the data never showed.

yield

The hard ones fail the gate more

An aimed draw keeps ≈ 54% of its conversations against ≈ 61% for a random one. The turns it drops are the late ones it was chosen for. N1's prefix rows only recover opening turns, so pair the aimed draw with N3: a gold-hinted repair is the only way a dropped conversation's late turns become rows.

mix

Keep a random base

Val and test are random draws. The 334 v1 rows stay in as the random base, and after the aimed 600 about half of v2's rows are long or hard conversations. Watch number turns on val (epoch 2: 83.2%). If they fall, add the random +347 as well: 91 of them are already in the aimed 600, so it adds 256 conversations for ≈ $0.46.

holdout

Aimed by val, not test

Test agrees on every weakness used here (program 70.6%, multiply 15%, question 5+ 64%). Negative gold answers look weak on test only (69.8% test, 71.9% val), so they get no weight; printed minus signs are left to T1 and N4. Val now steers the data as well as picking the checkpoint, so the next val gain will read a little high. Test settles it.

teacher

T1 · Fix the teacher's rules before collecting more

Three edits to the collection prompt's rules: a minus the table prints on a ratio, liability or loss is dropped before the arithmetic, while a computed difference always keeps its sign; "loss from X" returns the magnitude; the header value is the opening balance in a roll-forward table and the latest year in a history table. They touch ≈ 7 of 46 val and ≈ 6 of 45 test cases, but make a new teacher prompt, so N2's new rows and the old ones would disagree unless the 552 are re-collected (≈ $1.30). Do it before N2 if at all.

teacher

T2 · A stronger teacher only where Flash fails

Flash is the ceiling: 87.7% on test against the champion's 90.5%. Using V4-Pro or Sonnet only on conversations Flash got wrong would add hard rows without paying for the easy ones. This goes against "no more runs" for the challenger and Sonnet needs the subscription, so it is an option, not a recommendation, until N0–N3 have been measured.

free check

T3 · Try lower temperature on val first

All passes sampled at Qwen's recommended 0.7. A tuned student on an exact-answer task often does better near-greedy. One val pass of epoch 2 at temperature 0.2 costs ≈ 20 min of L4 and no training, and it could be part of the gain the next fine-tune is chasing. Change sampling for both arms or neither.

harness

H1–H3 · Harness checks, then re-score epoch 2 under them

H1 an extra calculator argument returns a retryable tool error, not a crash (4 test turns). H2 a program turn's answer must equal a calculator result (catches 4 wrong test turns, e.g. C/2009 answered 0.178 where the tool said 1.784). H3 calculator arguments follow the program's order (UNP/2013). They change the harness, so epoch 2 is re-scored on val under them and that becomes the baseline. Separately, run --resume-from refuses an open-runtime pass at effort "none" (the name carries -none, the check doesn't).
