why DPO fits
  • The mistakes are the student's own. Most first mistakes are wrong operand values or operands in the wrong order. Flash rarely makes them, so SFT on Flash's turns never showed them to the student (s31 §01).
  • Gold grades every try for free. A verified right try against a verified wrong one is the textbook pair for math-like tasks (rlhf-book ch11:L147-148).
  • SFT has plateaued. Val went flat after about one epoch, and sft2 against sft1's epoch 2 is +1.1 on test, not significant (s30).
DPO against the GRPO plan (s31, closed)
  • Offline. Sampling happens once, before training, so the pairs can be read and audited before a GPU trains on them.
  • The SFT stack is reused. The trainer is a training job like sft2's: the same image, the same mask code, an L4. No vLLM runs inside the trainer, and no weights are synced every step.
  • It can use the teacher where the student is always wrong. GRPO gets no signal from a question with no right try. DPO can use Flash's right answer as the chosen (D2).
what it gives up
  • The data goes stale. The pairs are drawn from sft2-e1. As training moves the student away from it, the pairs describe an older model. GRPO re-samples every step; DPO re-samples only if you run a second round.
  • Questions it always gets right teach nothing, exactly as for GRPO.
  • The book is silent on LoRA. Its DPO code is a full fine-tune (rlhf-book code/direct_alignment/train.py:L560-565). The LoRA settings here come from TRL's docs and from LoRA Without Regret.
02

How DPO works, from zero

SFT says "make the teacher's answer likely". DPO says "for the same prompt, make the right answer more likely relative to the wrong one than it was before training". It needs a pair of answers to one prompt, and a frozen copy of the starting model to measure "before" against.

The loss

L = −log σ( β · [ (log π(y⁺|x) − log π_ref(y⁺|x)) − (log π(y⁻|x) − log π_ref(y⁻|x)) ] )

  • x is the prompt: everything up to the current question. y⁺ is the chosen try, y⁻ the rejected one.
  • π is the student being trained. π_ref is the frozen starting point, sft2-e1.
  • β sets how far the student may move. A large β keeps it close to π_ref; a small β can over-optimise (rlhf-book lec6:L711-712). The usual value is 0.1, typically 0.1–0.5 (direct_alignment/loss.py:L76).
  • At step 0 the policy equals the reference, so the bracket is 0 and the loss is log 2 ≈ 0.693. It should fall from there.

A worked pair from val (illustration only)

Single_ETR/2008/page_153.pdf-2, q2: "what was, then, the change in amount over the year, in millions?" Gold is −17.4, subtract(19.2, 36.6). Two of the four sft2 checkpoints answered −17.4. The other two answered 17.4, with the operands reversed (s31 §02).

  • Chosen: a try that calls subtract(19.2, 36.6) and answers −17.4.
  • Rejected: a try that calls subtract(36.6, 19.2) and answers 17.4.
  • Tokens the two tries share, in the same context, add the same term to both sides and cancel. The gradient lands where they differ: the order of the operands.

Val picks checkpoints, so this pair is never trained on. The real pairs come from the fresh pool (§03).

What to update before sampling

  1. The harness retry budget stays as it is for sampling. backends/open_agent.py:170 builds Agent(…) without retries=, so its default of 1 shadows ToolOutput(max_retries=2), and a try can die on "Exceeded maximum output retries" (s31 P0, still unfixed). Building straight away means sampling on the harness sft2-e1 was scored on, so the tries match its val and test passes. A try the bug kills is dropped and counted. Fixing it means re-scoring the baseline, so the fix moves to P0's val passes.
  2. Add a sign check to the pair labeller, not to the eval metric. numeric_match rule 1 accepts any two numbers that round to the same integer, so 0.5 matches −0.5. 19 of 6,984 recorded answers scored right had the wrong sign (s31 §05). A wrong-sign "chosen" would teach exactly the wrong thing.
  3. Read the always-wrong questions for bad gold before any of them is paired with Flash's answer (D2).
  4. Cut a dpo_v1 manifest from the fresh pool, asserting sft_v2's page rules and leak checks. Hold back 5% of its conversations as fixed eval pairs, because per-step DPO metrics are mostly batch noise (rlhf-book direct_alignment/experiments/2026-02-08-orpo-simpo.md:L86, L91).
  5. Leave val and test alone. Val still picks checkpoints. Test 100 is opened once.
04

Building the chosen and rejected trajectories

A pair needs one prompt and two answers to it. In a conversation the prompt grows with every answer, so a pair can only be made one question at a time: the same history, then two different tries at the current question.

The recipe, per conversation

  1. Branch. At q0, send the same prompt to sft2-e1 4 times through the checked harness at the serving sampler (T 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20). open_agent._ask(agent, prompt, history) already answers one question on a given history; 4 calls on one history is a group. Every try is stored with the history it was asked on.
  2. Grade. A try is right if numeric_match accepts it and its sign agrees with gold. A try that errored with no answer is dropped, not rejected.
  3. Pair. If the group has both right and wrong tries, make up to 2 pairs from disjoint tries. Choose rejected tries with different wrong answers first, so two pairs cover two mistakes rather than one twice. DPO weights every pair equally (rlhf-book ch08:L243), and the cap keeps a 4-right/4-wrong question from contributing 16.
  4. Continue. Keep one right try, chosen at random, as the history and move to q1. Prefer one not used in a pair. The history is the student's own, as at serving. The book's multi-turn recipe also continues with the chosen answer (ch11:L137-140).
  5. Stop at a question with no right try. Later questions would rest on a wrong answer, and those knock-on errors are fixed by fixing the first mistake. With D2's rescue, Flash answers on the same history; if it's right it becomes the chosen and the conversation continues on it.
  6. Top up, later and only where it pays. Because each group's history is stored, more tries join the same group: about 4 more where all 4 were right (to find a flip) or all wrong (a right try lets the conversation continue past where it stopped), a few more where only one try was right or wrong (for a second pair), none where there are already two of each. Pairs are rebuilt over the merged group before training, with the same checkpoint, sampler and harness, and new seeds.

What one pair contains

  • Masked: the prompt, every tool reply, and any harness retry prompt. Tool outputs are the environment's words, not the model's (rlhf-book ch13:L212, L296). The two tries' tool replies differ, and scoring them would credit the student for the calculator's output.
  • Trained: the student's reasoning, its calculator calls and its StructuredOutput call, exactly the spans SFT trained. The same sft/rows.turn_messages renders them, and the same mask check proves it on every pair.
  • Excluded in round 1: any try that needed a harness retry (H1–H3). SFT collapses those detours, which would make a chosen differ from what the model actually wrote. The count is reported; if it's more than 15% of wrong tries, the rejected side learns to keep them, with the retry prompt masked.

The record

The training stack (D5)

recommended

B · Our own DPO step on the SFT stack

About 150 lines in dpo/train/dpo.py. It renders each side with sft/rows and the student's template, checks the mask with sft/train/mask.py, precomputes sft2-e1's log-probs, then runs the DPO + NLL step. It ships and resumes on Modal the way sft/train does.

Cost: we own the loss's correctness. Step-0 checks and a one-step test cover it.

fallback

A · TRL's DPOTrainer

It supports tool-calling conversations, PEFT models, precompute_ref_log_probs, and combined losses such as loss_type=["sigmoid","sft"].

Cost: three things need proving first. Its docs don't say whether tool replies inside a completion are masked. Whether a continued adapter's reference is sft2-e1 or the base model must be checked. And the 1,024 default must be overridden. For SFT, TRL also swapped in its own Qwen3 training template (s31 §06).

The RFT control arm

Same right tries, no rejected side, no reference model. Its loss is exactly the NLL half of DPO + NLL. The arms differ in one thing: whether the wrong tries are pushed down. The iterative reasoning paper ran the same control, one round of STaR (SFT on the chosen only), and it trailed DPO + NLL on GSM8K. Its Figure 2 suggests why: SFT on the chosen raised the rejected tries' log-probs too.

  8. P6 · Full run · ≈ $20–25. All pairs, at most 2 epochs, 2 val passes at each half-epoch checkpoint.
    stop early if val falls at two checkpoints in a row, or at the cap.
  9. P6r · RFT arm · ≈ $5–8. The chosen sides of the same train pairs, in its own Modal container, alongside P6. Same epochs, checkpoints and val passes.
    No pilot: its recipe is sft2's. Same early stop as P6. A step-0 check that --loss nll equals SFT cross-entropy on the chosen.
  10. P7 · Readout · ≈ $6. Each arm's val pick gets 4 passes. Compare both against sft2-e1's 4 and against each other, question by question, with a cluster bootstrap by conversation (§08 says how to read the result). Val decides between the arms. Test 100 is scored once, for the winning arm's pick only, on your word. Add rounds dpo1 and rft1 to evaluation/fine_tuning.json so Optimise › Fine-tuning shows both. Then a results page, a PR and no-mistakes.

Online DPO is exactly this, re-sampling from the current model (ch08:L265), and the iterative paper's gains came over several rounds.

08

How we'll know it worked

While it trains

  • Loss starts at 0.693 and should fall.
  • Rewards for chosen and rejected (β · log π/π_ref), their margin, and the share of eval pairs ranked right. The margin should grow without the samples collapsing (ch08:L346-347).
  • Chosen log-prob: if it falls steadily, that's likelihood displacement. The NLL term is there to stop it.
  • The length gap between chosen and rejected. If wrong tries tend to be shorter, DPO can learn length instead of correctness.
  • KL to sft2-e1 on eval pairs. A very large KL usually means a bug (ch14:L142).

Read these on the fixed eval pairs. Per-step numbers are mostly batch noise.

Choosing and comparing

  • Selection: 2 val passes at each checkpoint; the pick has the best mean.
  • Comparison: 4 passes of each arm's pick against sft2-e1's 4 from P0 and against each other, paired by question, with a cluster bootstrap by conversation. Accuracy by turn type, calculator calls per question, H1–H3 retry rate.
  • Test 100: once, for the winning arm's val pick, on your word. Its 95% CI is about ±3.5 points, so only a gain of about 4 or more reads as significant.
  • Audit: read 20 questions that went from wrong to right, and check that the reasoning, the program and the calculator calls support the answer.
